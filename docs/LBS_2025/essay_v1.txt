I faced casual discrimination a few times in my life. As I was raised in a rural part of India, I had limited access to the internet, only via cyber cafes. When I shifted to Pune, I started listening to English songs. I was only aware of famous pop songs on YouTube, and I used to listen to them. In the first year of my undergrad, my grasp of the English language wasn’t excellent, as I had been practising it for only a few years regularly. I used to make grammatical mistakes, my vocabulary was small. A few of my friends used to laugh at me for making mistakes. I didn’t try to hide my mistakes or feel bad about them; instead, I practised English more with friends who used to point out my mistakes, which helped me get more grasp on the language. 
Over time, I have learned criticism well, whether it is positive or negative. Whenever anyone criticizes me, my first response is to understand their point of view and gauge whether there is something that I can take out from it to improve myself. So even if anyone is providing me constructive criticism with negative delivery, I don’t get affected and get the best out of it.
