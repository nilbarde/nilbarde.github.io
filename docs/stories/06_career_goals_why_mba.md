CURRENT-ROLE EVIDENCE - WHY MBA, WHY NOW (2026 UPDATE)

The case for MBA timing has become sharper since the original goals above were written, driven by two patterns visible in the current role at Derq.

**Pattern 1: Earning the role by doing the role.**

At Derq today, I am doing product-shaped work inside an engineering title. On the most recent flagship project (Conflict Prediction for Ohio DoT, see `04e_*`), I was Lead Developer on paper and PM-cum-tech-lead in practice — Arijit gave me full client-interaction freedom, and I led requirements, scope, technical direction, and delivery. On API Integration (`04b_*`) I led client calls independently while a peer needed PM support consistently. On ATSPM (`04c_*`) I questioned conventional wisdom and conducted direct client interviews "rarely done at this level" per Arijit. The shape of the work has been PM for some time; the title and credibility lag.

The Montreal DOT crisis sprint (`04f_*`) is the sharpest single instance of this pattern. Pulled in cold for the final 10 business days of an inherited offline-port project, with no formal authority over Full Stack or product, I ended up the de-facto delivery coordinator for the sprint — the PM (Arijit) and the Full Stack team lead routed client-build-stage decisions through me directly. After deployment, when leadership asked who should walk the CTO and HR through the planning failure, the answer was me — *one long call with the CTO and three with HR*, all on systemic process improvement. The seat I occupied during that sprint was structural; nothing about my title justified it. That is the most concrete possible evidence of "doing the role before holding it."

The NDS video-processing project (`04g_*`) extends this to the business-strategy layer. What started as defending an estimate ended with the CTO and other executives discussing *strategy* with me directly — whether to pursue this class of work, what the risks are, how to deliver faster than competitors. An IC's judgment call became the trigger for a productization decision, and the IC got pulled into the room where that decision is being shaped. Note the honest attribution: the senior title, the compensation increase, and the expanded product say didn't come from this one project — they came from consistently delivering in a collaborative environment plus a pattern of courageous calls, of which NDS is one example. The point isn't "one project earned a promotion"; it's that the *consistent pattern* is now visible enough that I'm doing strategy-shaped work without strategy-grade frameworks.

This is the same pattern in miniature that the MBA is meant to address at industry scale. Inside Derq the gap closes through a promotion conversation. Outside Derq, the title gap is real — and the MBA is the most credible lever for closing it.

**Pattern 2: The structural-insight gap.**

In the current scope, I see a recurring structural problem: three teams (product project, ML, engineering) operate in sequence rather than in parallel. Requirements reach engineering after key decisions are already locked in. The result is avoidable rework, slower ML-to-production cycles, and missed opportunities to convert custom client work into reusable product capability.

I can articulate the problem. I have built reusable patterns that mitigate it (the API integration framework in `04b_*`, the real-time QA verification pattern in `04e_*`, the SigLIP module standardization in Fire Detection). I have a working intuition for the **reusability ratio** — how much of one-off client work converts into core product capability vs disappearing into one-off custom code.

Montreal sharpened this pattern further. During the crisis sprint I surfaced an architectural gap that had been latent in the product for ~8 years: the backend software had no proper API surface for management/configuration interactions. Cloud-side tooling had papered over it for years; an offline deployment made it structural. I documented it, presented it to the CTO with severity-by-risk-horizon framing, and was asked to lead the research and proposal for fixing it (see `04f_*` for the full story). This is the structural-insight pattern operating in its cleanest form — see the foundational shape, surface it from inside the work, get handed the redesign.

NDS (`04g_*`) is the cleanest instance of the reusability-ratio intuition paying off at the business level. A category the org had deprioritized as one-off custom work for years — recorded-video processing — I argued (and then demonstrated under a deadline) was worth converting into core product capability. The org reversed the deprioritization and is now productizing it. I made that conversion call on instinct; what I lacked was the framework to value it, size the market, and argue it at the executive level *before* a crisis forced the answer.

What I don't have is the **formal toolkit** to redesign cross-team workflows at scale, the financial frameworks to value the reusability decisions I'm making intuitively, or the credibility to argue for those redesigns at the level above my current role. Today my structural-insight reach ends at the edge of the technical surface; the MBA is what extends it to strategy, market, and org questions with the same confidence. That is precisely what the MBA's strategy, operations, and finance curricula are designed to provide.

**Pattern 3: The least-experienced-in-the-room throughline (want, not need).**

There is a single pattern underneath my whole trajectory: I keep choosing rooms where I start behind, and I climb by learning from everyone in them. It is not a one-off; it is the operating system of the journey.

- **Shiroda → Pune → IIT Bombay → Dubai.** Each move dropped me into a more competitive environment than the last — a small hometown to metro coaching, to IIT, to an international company abroad (the relocations in `07_*`, the JEE drop-year reset in `01_*`). Every time I entered as one of the most basic people in the room.
- **IIT Bombay.** Youngest member who became the lead, directing Master's and PhD students older and more credentialed than me (`02_*`, `05_*`).
- **Tvarit.** Walked in as an ML engineer and *earned* the PM role and the respect — Technical Person and Employee of the Quarter (`03_*`).
- **Derq.** Did it again; Arijit's LOR is the external proof.

The important half is that I do not *stay* behind. The arc is consistent: start as the most basic person in the room, learn from everyone, end up earning and often leading it. "I want to be where everyone knows more than me" is therefore not a humility pose — it is the environment in which I have, every single time, grown fastest.

This is why the MBA is a **want, not a need.** I do not need it to save my career — I just earned a Senior Software Developer title, I have a strong path forward as a developer, and the CTO already brings strategy to me (`04g_*`). I am choosing the harder room on purpose. The MBA classroom is simply the next instance of the same pattern: peers with more years, real manager/PM titles, and more hands-on management experience than I have. I want to put myself there precisely because I will not be the best in it — that is where I have always become better. The downside is real (I might fall; I will not be the smartest), and I am choosing it anyway, because the want here is a need of a different kind: to reach the dream, to feed the hunger to learn, and to build innovations that help people at scale.

**Why now, sharper.**

I have stretched the technical-ownership lane about as far as it goes — owning ~10 parallel modules, being the connective tissue across functions, originating reusable patterns that other engineers now build against. The promotion conversation at Derq is essentially about formalizing contributions I'm already making. The MBA is the equivalent move at industry scale: the credentialing for the work I'm already doing, plus the formal frameworks to do it consistently rather than instinctively, plus the network to scale it beyond Derq.

Another year of the same work won't unlock the things I'm reaching for. The MBA is the lever.
