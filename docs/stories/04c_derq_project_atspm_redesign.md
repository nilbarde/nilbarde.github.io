BACKGROUND - ATSPM SYSTEM

ATSPM (Automated Traffic Signal Performance Measures) is a system that helps traffic engineers analyze how well their traffic signals are performing. It collects data from traffic signals and generates performance metrics to answer questions like:
- Are signals causing unnecessary delays?
- Are emergency vehicles getting through intersections quickly?
- Are pedestrians getting adequate crossing time?
- Which intersections need timing adjustments?

This is critical for city traffic management—poor signal timing wastes fuel, increases emissions, frustrates drivers, and can create safety hazards.

USER RESEARCH & PAIN POINT IDENTIFICATION

For this product, I worked closely with Product Manager Arijit Bose and collaborated with traffic engineers from various U.S. Departments of Transportation (DoTs) to gather insights and feedback.

Through in-depth interviews and workshops with the engineers, I identified several key pain points:

**Integration Complexity:**
- Engineers expressed concerns about difficulty integrating ATSPM systems with existing traffic management infrastructure
- Different cities had different legacy systems
- Integration often required extensive system overhauls
- This complexity created adoption barriers

**Accuracy Limitations:**
- Current performance metrics had accuracy issues
- Generic algorithms didn't account for local traffic patterns
- Engineers didn't fully trust the data for making decisions
- This undermined the system's entire value proposition

**Poor User Experience:**
- Engineers complained about unintuitive interfaces
- Data was presented but not actionable
- Unclear insights made it hard to know what actions to take
- User-unfriendly design meant systems went unused even when deployed

QUESTIONING CONVENTIONAL WISDOM

When we began the ATSPM redesign, the path forward seemed clear to everyone. Engineering managers and peers recommended implementing existing open-source ATSPM solutions to deliver quickly—a pragmatic approach that would save development time and leverage proven technology.

This was the conventional wisdom: **why reinvent the wheel when free, well-tested alternatives already exist?**

But I asked a different question that reframed the entire project:

**"If free, open-source alternatives already exist and work well, why aren't our clients using them?"**

This simple question revealed a fundamental gap in our understanding. The issue wasn't technical availability—it was whether existing solutions actually solved the underlying client problems. Rather than assuming I knew the answer, I went directly to the source.

RESEARCH-DRIVEN REDESIGN APPROACH

I conducted direct client interviews with traffic engineers across different U.S. Departments of Transportation—something Arijit later noted was "rarely done by product managers at my level."

These weren't surface-level conversations about feature requests. They were deep explorations of why clients found existing solutions inadequate despite their technical capabilities.

The research revealed that clients needed more than just functionality:

**Accuracy Improvements:**
- Open-source solutions couldn't deliver because of generic algorithms
- Local traffic patterns require customized approaches
- Generic = good enough for research, insufficient for operational decisions

**Seamless Integration:**
- Not just theoretical compatibility, but actually working with their specific existing systems
- Each city's infrastructure is unique
- Off-the-shelf solutions require extensive customization anyway

**Intuitive Interfaces:**
- Teams could actually use without extensive training
- Information architecture matters as much as features
- Design for actual users (traffic engineers), not generic users

**Actionable Insights:**
- Not just raw data visualization
- Recommendations on what actions to take
- Prioritization of which intersections need attention first

This understanding transformed our approach. Where my peers would have optimized incrementally by tweaking open-source solutions, I redesigned from first principles based on actual user needs.

Arijit captured this distinction in his recommendation: "Where his colleagues would have optimised incrementally, his comprehensive understanding transformed the product into a market differentiator that created long-term business value instead of just solving immediate technical problems."

I conducted competitive analysis, reviewed case studies, and examined data from existing deployments—not to copy what existed, but to understand why it fell short.

THREE REDESIGN PRIORITIES

Using this data-driven approach, I emphasized three key priorities in the redesign:

**1. System Flexibility:**
I ensured the system could seamlessly integrate with a wide range of existing infrastructure, reducing the burden on engineers to make extensive system overhauls.

This wasn't about being compatible with every possible system (impossible)—it was about making integration straightforward for the most common configurations while providing clear extension points for unique cases.

**2. Data Accuracy:**
I worked with both the technical team and subject matter experts to implement algorithms that would provide more accurate performance metrics, enabling engineers to make better-informed decisions.

We moved from generic algorithms to approaches that could be calibrated for local conditions. This required more upfront work but delivered accuracy improvements that justified the investment.

**3. User Experience (UX):**
I redesigned the interface with actionable insights and visualizations, prioritizing usability to ensure traffic engineers could efficiently monitor and adjust traffic signals in real-time.

This meant working with actual traffic engineers to understand their workflow, what decisions they make, and what information they need at what times.

POST-REDESIGN OWNERSHIP - REPORTS SUITE CONTINUES

The ATSPM redesign was not a one-and-done project. Since the ground-up redesign delivered flagship status and 15% faster revenue target achievement, I have continued to own **development and improvement of all reports in the ATSPM suite** as part of the broader long-term role at Derq.

This continued ownership matters for three reasons:

1. **The redesign as a durable product.** Flagship status is meaningful only if the underlying product keeps pace with client demand. Ongoing reports development is what keeps ATSPM at the front of the portfolio rather than letting it decay into legacy.
2. **A stable platform for new client onboarding.** Because the reports suite is mature and continuously improved, new DoT clients can be brought on with confidence in the deliverable quality — the redesign's payoff compounds with every new client.
3. **A bridge to other modules.** Insights from ATSPM client interactions feed into adjacent modules (Conflict Prediction, TNL Report, V2X). ATSPM is increasingly a beachhead, not just a standalone product.

The original redesign answered "why don't clients use free alternatives?" Continued ownership answers a different question: "how do you keep a flagship product flagship?" The answer turns out to be the same instinct — research-driven changes rooted in actual client workflows, rather than feature-by-feature optimization.
