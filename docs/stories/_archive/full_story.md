The next project I’m working on is focused on detecting near-miss and crash events, which are critical for enhancing road safety. Near-miss events are defined as situations where two objects come very close to colliding but do not actually make contact. These events typically occur at intersections in high-traffic areas, where cameras are deployed to monitor the surroundings. In these locations, crashes can either occur or near-miss events can be captured between two vehicles or between a vehicle and a vulnerable road user (VRU).
I am collaborating with Product Manager Arijit Bose, Project Lead Usman, and QA Engineer Bilal on this project. Our team is focused on developing a system on the backend to detect these high-risk events from live camera feeds. In parallel, we are engaging in discussions with clients to define the severity of these events—distinguishing between high and low-severity incidents. Understanding the severity is crucial, as it helps the client take appropriate actions to mitigate such risks and improve road safety.
Based on these client discussions and our research, we are developing a safety scoring system and generating heatmaps to highlight areas with the highest frequency of near-miss or crash events. This allows clients to easily identify hotspots and take targeted actions to improve safety at critical locations. As each client has unique requirements, we continuously iterate on the system and refine our dashboard to meet their specific needs and help them enhance the safety of road users in their respective areas.
Since the requirements for this project are highly subjective and vary across different clients, we are engaged in ongoing research to improve the system and adapt our solutions to support their goals better. Our ultimate aim is to provide clients with a tool that proactively identifies near-miss events and enables them to improve safety based on actionable insights.
The next project I worked on involved integrating our system with clients' monitoring platforms through API calls. Most of our clients are state governments in the US and Canada, particularly their Departments of Transportation (DoTs), who manage control rooms for monitoring traffic conditions. These control rooms rely on manual monitoring of camera feeds to detect various safety hazards, such as stopped vehicles on highways, near-miss events, crashes, or pedestrians walking on roads. However, it is not feasible for the engineers in these control rooms to monitor all cameras continuously or respond in real-time to every event.
Our system detects various types of events and displays the details on a dashboard. Since the engineers cannot monitor our dashboard at all times, the solution we proposed was to send real-time API calls to their systems. This way, important event details could be displayed directly on the engineers' screens as part of their existing monitoring workflow.
I worked closely with Product Manager Arijit Bose to ensure our solution met the engineers' needs. We interviewed traffic engineers from multiple municipalities, such as Multiplex Kies, to understand their requirements for receiving API calls. The engineers had different preferences for transmitting the data: some preferred API calls over the internet, while others preferred local network calls. Additionally, depending on the client, we had to account for varying security protocols, such as the need for login credentials, secret keys, or even customized API call receivers.
After gathering these diverse requirements, I led the development of the API integration module, ensuring compatibility with various client protocols and security measures. Once the module was deployed, we held feedback sessions with the engineers to gather insights on improving the system. A common request was to include a link to a video feed alongside the event details, enabling engineers to visually verify the situation before taking action.
This new product not only improved real-time monitoring for our clients but also opened the door for interactions with potential new clients who required similar capabilities. Currently, the product is in the post-deployment support phase, where we make updates and improvements only when new clients request specific changes or introduce new protocols for the API calls.
The next product I worked on was the ATSPM (Automated Traffic Signal Performance Measures) system. For this product, I worked closely with the company’s Product Manager, Arijit Bose, and collaborated with traffic engineers from various U.S. Departments of Transportation (DoTs) to gather insights and feedback.
Through in-depth interviews and workshops with the engineers, I was able to identify several key pain points and opportunities for improvement. Many engineers expressed concerns regarding the complexity of integration with existing traffic management systems, as well as limitations in the accuracy of current performance matrices. Additionally, user feedback emphasized the need for more intuitive and user-friendly interfaces that could provide clearer insights into traffic signal performance.
Based on these insights, I led the effort to redesign the service from the ground up. My research focused on understanding the specific challenges faced by traffic engineers in managing real-world traffic data and performance metrics. I conducted competitive analysis, reviewed case studies, and examined data from existing deployments to better understand the gaps in current solutions.
Using this data-driven approach, I emphasized three key priorities in the redesign:
System Flexibility: I ensured the system could seamlessly integrate with a wide range of existing infrastructure, reducing the burden on engineers to make extensive system overhauls.
Data Accuracy: I worked with both the technical team and subject matter experts to implement algorithms that would provide more accurate performance metrics, enabling engineers to make better-informed decisions.
User Experience (UX): I redesigned the interface with actionable insights and visualizations, prioritizing usability to ensure traffic engineers could efficiently monitor and adjust traffic signals in real-time.
The redesigned ATSPM system significantly improved data accuracy and delivered more actionable performance metrics than competing solutions in the market. This differentiation not only addressed our clients' pain points but also helped position the product as a flagship offering in our company’s portfolio.
