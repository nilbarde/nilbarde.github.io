JOINING DERQ - COMPANY BACKGROUND

After being an integral part of Tvarit for 14 months, I joined my current US-based company, Derq, where I have been working for the past 2.5 years from the Dubai office. Derq is focused on increasing road safety for all users through AI-powered computer vision systems that analyze traffic patterns and detect safety hazards in real-time.

Our primary client base consists of state and city Departments of Transportation (DoTs) in the US and Canada, with expanding operations in the Middle East (Dubai, Abu Dhabi) and pilot projects in Saudi Arabia and Europe. We work with government agencies to deploy camera-based systems that detect various safety events: stopped vehicles, near-miss incidents, crashes, pedestrians in dangerous locations, and traffic signal performance issues.

U.S. DOT INTERSECTION SAFETY CHALLENGE VICTORY

Derq's engineering team recently won the Intersection Safety Challenge (ISC) organized by the U.S. Department of Transportation. This national competition focused on developing innovative solutions to improve intersection safety—one of the most dangerous aspects of road infrastructure, where the majority of traffic accidents occur.

Our team's submission showcased our AI-powered road safety platform's ability to:

- Analyze traffic patterns to identify high-risk intersections

This win validated our technical approach and positioned Derq as a leader in the intersection safety space, opening doors to new government contracts and partnerships.

ISC COMPETITION - CRISIS MANAGEMENT UNDER PRESSURE

During the Intersection Safety Challenge competition, we faced an unexpected crisis that tested our team's resilience and my leadership under pressure. The competition organizers suddenly changed the required data format mid-competition—a significant technical challenge that could have derailed our entire submission and months of preparation.

While most team members reacted with panic, uncertain how to proceed with this unexpected setback, I maintained composure and focused on the solution rather than the problem. The crisis revealed a leadership principle I've carried since my IIT Bombay days: technical excellence matters, but emotional intelligence determines success when the unexpected happens.

Rather than placing blame on the organizers or expressing frustration at the disruption, I calmly supported our systems engineer who was responsible for adapting to the format change. I provided reassurance that we could solve this, worked alongside him to troubleshoot the technical issues systematically, and stayed late into the night until we successfully resolved the problem and submitted our entry on time.

This crisis became an opportunity to demonstrate that leadership isn't just about technical skills—it's about maintaining team morale and effectiveness during high-pressure situations when stakes are highest. When others panicked, I became the stabilizing force that kept our team focused and productive.

My Senior Product Manager, Arijit Bose (Oxford MBA), later noted in his recommendation: "This ability to maintain team effectiveness during high-pressure situations, when others are overwhelmed, demonstrates emotional maturity that sets him apart from colleagues and contributes significantly to successful project outcomes."

The $750,000 victory validated not just our technical solution, but our team's ability to perform under extreme pressure and adapt to unexpected challenges—qualities essential for any high-stakes business environment.

**ML-integrated feature development:**

- **Image Quality Analyzer** — video feed quality assessment module.
- **Conflict Prediction** — real-time conflict prediction at Ohio DoT's Dublin roundabout; flagship use case (see `04e_*` for the full project story).
- **TNL Report** — cross-signal conflict detection within a corridor.

**The shape of the role today.** Across these modules, the position has shifted from "module developer" to **cross-module engineering anchor**: the engineering point of contact for clients on multiple analytics modules, the bridge between the ML team (research outputs), the product project team (client requirements), and QA (quality gates). The connective-tissue role increasingly defines the day-to-day more than any single module does.

**A structural observation about the org.** Three teams today operate in sequence rather than in parallel: the product project team gathering client requirements, the ML team producing research, and the engineering team translating both into production. Requirements often reach engineering after key decisions are already locked in, which creates avoidable rework and slows ML-to-production cycles. The expanded role is positioned to close that gap by embedding senior engineering perspective earlier in the workflow.

**Company direction context.** Derq is shifting from client-acquisition-first to product-first and reorganizing function-based teams (software, ML, FS) into product-based teams. Analytics-team placement is the most likely landing inside the new structure. The aim is to be recognized as the senior technical anchor *across* modules rather than competing for any specific team-lead title.
