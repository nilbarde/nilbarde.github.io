FAMILY & FRIENDS SUPPORT FOR MBA DECISION

QUESTION: We encourage applicants to take into consideration the impact of study on family and friends. Who have you discussed your plans with and what was the response?

FAMILY SUPPORT & BACKGROUND

I have discussed my intention to do an MBA with my family and friends, and all of them responded with encouragement and excitement. As the first person from my family and village to attend a prestigious institute like IIT Bombay, my achievements have been a source of pride for my parents. They feel their sacrifices have been worthwhile and their efforts justified.

My family has been very well adjusted to my staying away from home to pursue opportunities since 2014—first for JEE preparation in Pune, then engineering at IIT Bombay in Mumbai, and presently for work in Dubai. My mother, father, elder brother, and sister-in-law have consistently supported my academic and professional choices through this decade-long journey.

With my brother and father each running their own shops independently, there are no financial dependencies on me that would hinder my focus on studies. More importantly, my work over the past few years has empowered me to clear my student debts, allowing me to pursue the MBA as an investment in my future without burdening my parents financially. This financial independence means I can dedicate myself fully to business education without creating strain on my family.

MENTORS & EARLY INFLUENCES

My childhood mentor, Sagar sir, who once dreamed of seeing me at a world-class university, was emotionally overcome when I showed him my IIT degree—seeing his village student reach IIT Bombay fulfilled a dream he'd held for years. He is optimistic about my decision to pursue an MBA, believing that it will open up more opportunities for me to inspire and empower students from our village to pursue their own dreams. His support carries special weight—he sees my education not just as personal achievement but as a beacon for our entire community.

COUSINS' INSPIRATION & INFLUENCE

My cousins, despite the distance, are quite close; they, too, find my academic path an inspiration. They at times ask for advice for their academic pursuits and say my plans for an MBA have taken them further in considering postgraduate education. One cousin who has experienced the benefits of an MBA himself offered practical advice about balancing opportunities with challenges—the intense academic demands, time away from work, and significant investment required.

FRIENDS' ENCOURAGEMENT & NETWORK VALUE

My friends, also mainly from technical backgrounds, have always been encouraging me to pursue an MBA. They recognise my leadership qualities and management potential, often encouraging me to transition from purely technical roles to broader business responsibilities. They see a lot of value in my choice of pursuing business education over technical masters since this will be a valuable addition to their network. While our current circle is very technical, my presence in business school will create invaluable bridges between the technical and management spheres to benefit our whole network.

SUMMARY

Therefore, this decision meets not only my personal objectives but also gains full support from my family and friends, who view it as a logical sequence in my career development.

REFLECTION:

This question reveals an important dimension of MBA readiness that transcends academics and professional achievements: **support systems and life context**.

**Family Foundation**: The fact that my family has supported my being away from home since 2014 (JEE preparation → IIT Bombay → Dubai) demonstrates a decade-long pattern of independence and family trust. This isn't a sudden leap into unfamiliarity—it's the continuation of a journey my family has been part of from the beginning. Their excitement about the MBA reflects understanding that education and growth sometimes require physical distance, but the relationships remain strong.

The financial independence (father and brother running their own shops) is crucial. Unlike situations where family members depend on my income, I have complete freedom to invest 1-2 years in education without causing hardship. This removes a major source of stress that some MBA students face and allows me to focus entirely on maximizing the program's value.

**Cousins as Inspiration**: The fact that my MBA plans inspire cousins to consider postgraduate education reveals an important dynamic—**my educational choices have ripple effects beyond my own career**. This creates positive pressure to succeed (others are watching and being influenced) and reinforces the importance of the decision. It's not just about my own development; it's about modeling possibilities for extended family.

**Friends' Strategic Perspective**: My friends' enthusiasm is particularly telling because it's not sentimental—it's strategic. They explicitly recognize that:
1. I have leadership qualities beyond technical execution
2. MBA is more valuable than technical masters given my skills
3. My business education benefits our entire network

The "bridge between technical and management spheres" comment is insightful. Our current network is homogeneous (technical). My MBA creates heterogeneity, connecting our technical community to business perspectives, opportunities, and collaborations. This network effect amplifies the MBA's value beyond my individual learning.

**Logical Progression, Not Pivot**: The most important signal is that family and friends view this as "logical sequence in career development," not a risky change. They've observed my journey (IIT technical leadership → Tvarit PM role → Derq product innovation) and recognize that the MBA is the next natural step, not a departure from my path.
