JOINING IIT BOMBAY - INNOVATION CELL SELECTION

After joining IIT Bombay, I was introduced to various opportunities in technical and art clubs. Among these, the Innovation Cell caught my attention, especially because of its work on self-driving cars. I participated in their entrance exam and selection process and was chosen to join the team. Impressed by my contributions to a Junior project, the team invited me to join the self-driving car project after my first semester, making me the youngest member.

YEAR 1 SUMMER - MUMBAI DATA COLLECTION INITIATIVE

During my summer break after the first year, I proposed and led the initiative to collect data for road lane detection, traffic signs, and traffic light detection from various parts of Mumbai. Recognizing the time limitations, I coordinated with my team, assigning members to collect data from areas we couldn't reach by car. I also designed custom annotation tools, enabling us to generate the data necessary for training machine learning models efficiently.

COMPUTER VISION EXCELLENCE & DECISION-MAKING TEAM

In the following months, I worked on several computer vision projects, delivering some of the best results the team had seen to date. My ability to think critically and my commitment to excellence led to an invitation to join the decision-making team, where I continued to contribute in a leadership capacity. In my second year, I played a key role in the on-ground testing of the self-driving car, collaborating closely with a professor to enhance the computer vision models used in the project. I was honoured with an Institute Technical Special Mention from the Student Gymkhana for my technical contributions, recognizing my impact in the field.

SEMESTER 5 - LEAD POSITION & FINANCIAL MANAGEMENT

In my fifth semester, I was entrusted with the lead position for the decision-making and computer vision subsystems of the self-driving car project and the car's testing process. In addition, I took on the responsibility of managing the finances of our technical club. My efforts resulted in securing 30 lakhs INR for research projects, the highest funding awarded to any technical club at the institute.

STRATEGIC PARTNERSHIPS & SPONSORSHIPS

My team and I also secured sponsorships for five lidars from Ouster and Velodyne and high-performance GPUs from Nvidia. We were granted access to a testing arena at the Reliance Jio campus in Navi Mumbai, where we conducted on-ground testing for the self-driving car.

INTER-IIT & COLLABORATIVE RESEARCH

The institute's technical council invited me to participate in their inter-IIT competition, where I contributed to a project focused on autonomous bots. Additionally, at the request of Professor Amit Sethi, who guided our self-driving car project, I mentored an MTech student through his research thesis. I also collaborated with two other professors: one on a generative AI research project and the other for a Spotify competition to predict user behaviour.

PROFESSOR AMIT SETHI - THE LIFELONG LEARNER LESSON

Professor Amit Sethi, a trailblazer at IIT Bombay and pioneer in bringing AI into the academic realm, served as our faculty advisor for the self-driving car project. Beyond our regular interactions about team performance and future plans, one particular incident profoundly shaped my approach to learning and mentorship.

Professor Sethi asked me to assist post-graduate (MTech) students in writing their final projects. I foolishly presumed it was because of my particular skill set—my computer vision expertise or technical abilities. As I took this forward, it gradually dawned on me that there was more at play.

While assisting these senior students, I came to learn how one can expand their horizons beyond just technical skills. I had considered managing a team an achievement in its own right, but this experience taught me that there is always scope to hone, and there would always be something to learn from others—even when you are the one who is mentoring.

This revelation transformed how I view relationships. Looking back, some of my most meaningful connections in life have been because I reached out to either mentor or be mentored. The boundaries between teacher and student, mentor and mentee, became fluid. Every mentorship became a two-way learning opportunity.

In his own wisdom, Professor Sethi ensured that I would be a lifelong learner. He taught me intellectual humility: the understanding that expertise in one area doesn't mean mastery of all areas, and that the act of teaching often reveals how much more there is to learn.

COVID-19 PERIOD - SKILL DEVELOPMENT & MENTORSHIP

The COVID-19 lockdown during my sixth and seventh semesters provided an unexpected opportunity for personal growth and skill development. While most of my peers struggled with the sudden shift to online learning, I took the initiative to learn new technologies, including Python GUI applications, Kivy, PyQt, and Django. This self-driven learning not only expanded my technical toolkit but also helped me stay connected with my passion for coding and development during a challenging time.

In parallel, I dedicated time to helping junior members of my team improve their skills in machine learning and decision-making. I organized virtual sessions where I shared knowledge on key topics, answered their questions, and provided guidance on personal projects. I also mentored them on how to approach problem-solving in real-world applications, using decision-making frameworks that I had learned during my time at IIT Bombay. Through this, I strengthened my own understanding while giving back to the team and fostering a collaborative learning environment during a period of isolation.

FINAL SEMESTER - MAHINDRA RISE COMPETITION WIN

In my last semester at IIT Bombay, our team was granted special permission to return to campus and continue work on the self-driving car project, as it was one of the institute's most important initiatives. We prepared a detailed technical report and created testing videos, which we submitted to the Mahindra Rise competition—a challenge focused on developing self-driving cars for Indian road conditions. Our team achieved the best results among all competitors and was awarded the first prize of 25 lakh INR.

INTER-IIT TRAFFIC SIGN RECOGNITION COMPETITION

In the same semester, I participated in two additional competitions. The first, Traffic Sign Recognition, was part of an inter-IIT tech summit. The technical council gave me full autonomy to select my team and make all key decisions throughout the competition. I led an interview process to select ten members from 75 candidates, managed the team's workflow, collaborated with the UI/UX specialists to finalize the website design, and provided technical guidance. The team won bronze and silver medals for our performance.

ASME STUDENT DESIGN CHALLENGE - WORLD FINALS

The second competition was the Student Design Challenge, organized by ASME (the American Society of Mechanical Engineers). As the lead, I coordinated the design and development of two solar-powered bots—one autonomous and the other manually controlled—with wireless communication between the two. I managed the technical planning, secured funding and presentation space from the institute, and oversaw the team's work. Our efforts earned us 4th place in the world finals.
